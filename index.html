Song Guo (Chinese: 郭嵩) is a computer scientist known for his work on edge computing and edge artificial intelligence, distributed systems, machine-learning systems, and physical AI. He is a chair professor and associate head for research and knowledge transfer in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST).[1] In 2024, the IEEE Computer Society recognized his “pioneering contributions to edge computing and edge intelligence technologies” with the Edward J. McCluskey Technical Achievement Award.[2] In 2026, he was elected to the Academy of Science of the Royal Society of Canada and received the CCF Award for Overseas Outstanding Contributions.[3][4][5]

Biography

Guo received a bachelor's degree in computer engineering from the Huazhong University of Science and Technology in 1995 and a master's degree in computer engineering from the Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications in 1998. He completed a PhD in computer science at the University of Ottawa.[6]

After a postdoctoral fellowship at the University of British Columbia in 2006, he was an assistant professor at the University of Northern British Columbia from 2006 to 2007. He joined the University of Aizu in 2007, becoming a full professor there in 2014. From 2016 to 2023, he was a professor at the Hong Kong Polytechnic University, where he directed the Edge Intelligence Lab.[6][7] In 2019, he received a Changjiang Chair Professorship (長江學者講座教授) from China's Ministry of Education.[8] He joined HKUST in 2023. In addition to his departmental roles, HKUST lists him as director of its Center of Physical AI and program director of its MSc in Artificial Intelligence.[9]

Research

Guo's research spans distributed and cloud computing, mobile computing, edge computing, and machine-learning systems.[1][9] His work on edge intelligence has examined how model training and inference can be moved from centralized cloud infrastructure toward edge servers and end devices. The IEEE Computer Society has described his contributions as including the development of edge learning, a machine-learning paradigm for training and inference at the network edge.[15]

In the edge-learning model described by Guo, data generated by mobile and Internet-of-things devices can be used for local or collaborative model training, while inference is performed closer to the point of data collection. The approach is intended to reduce reliance on centralized processing, lower communication latency, and improve privacy and real-time responsiveness.[15]

His later research has expanded to large language models, world models, embodied AI, and physical AI. At HKUST, he directs the Pervasive Intelligence Laboratory, which develops architectures, algorithms, and systems for robotic agents, world models, embodied AI, and foundation models.[16] His work on edge-native foundation-model services investigates the use of distributed and heterogeneous edge resources to provide lower-latency, privacy-preserving access to foundation models.[17]

Achievements

Guo was named a Clarivate Highly Cited Researcher in computer science for seven consecutive years from 2020 through 2026.[6][18][8][19] The designation identifies researchers who have produced multiple papers ranking in the top one percent by citations for their field and publication year in the Web of Science.[18] HKUST also reports that he has received more than a dozen best-paper awards from IEEE and ACM venues.[8]

The IEEE Computer Society gave Guo its 2024 Edward J. McCluskey Technical Achievement Award for pioneering contributions to edge computing and edge intelligence technologies.[2] He directed the Edge Intelligence Lab at Hong Kong Polytechnic University from 2016 to 2023 and later became director of HKUST's Center of Physical AI and Physical Edge Intelligence Lab, as well as program director of its MSc in Artificial Intelligence.[6][9]

In 2019, the IEEE Computer Society appointed Guo the inaugural editor-in-chief of the IEEE Open Journal of the Computer Society, beginning with the journal's launch in 2020.[10] He later became editor-in-chief of IEEE Transactions on Cloud Computing.[11]

His professional recognition includes election as an IEEE Fellow, a Fellow of the Canadian Academy of Engineering, a member of Academia Europaea, and an elected Fellow of the Royal Society of Canada.[14][13][7][3]

Professional service

Societies and editorial service

Guo's professional-society service has included membership of the IEEE Communications Society Board of Governors, director of its membership services, chair of its Technical Committee on Satellite and Space Communications, membership of IEEE Fellow evaluation committees, and leadership roles in CCF Hong Kong and the Global Academy of Overseas Chinese Scholars.[6] His CV lists 14 journal editorships or editorial-board appointments. These include editor-in-chief of IEEE Transactions on Cloud Computing, Edge Intelligence and Systems, Journal of Intelligent Computing and Networking, and the IEEE Open Journal of the Computer Society, as well as editorial roles for ACM Computing Surveys, IEEE Transactions on Computers, IEEE Transactions on Parallel and Distributed Systems, IEEE Internet of Things Journal, and IEEE Network.[6][10][11]

Review panels

His CV records 13 review-panel or funding-review appointments. They include two terms on engineering panels of the Hong Kong Research Grants Council; the selection committee of the Asian Young Scientist Fellowship; the academic committee of the Hong Kong Scholars Program; and reviewing for Singapore's Ministry of Education Academic Research Fund, Canada's Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council, the National Natural Science Foundation of China, China's national science and technology expert system, Belgium's FNRS, the Dutch Research Council, Luxembourg's National Research Fund, Poland's National Science Centre, and the Wellcome Trust.[6]

Conference organization and program committees

The CV lists 73 major conference-organization appointments between 2008 and 2026, including general, program, track, symposium, and workshop chair roles. Selected appointments include general chair of WASA 2026, ChinaCom and IEEE BDCloud in 2024, IEEE VTC 2023-Fall, IEEE CLOUD 2020, IEEE IC2E 2019, and IEEE CyberSciTech 2017; track chair of ICDCS 2026; TPC co-chair of ICNC 2026 and IEEE VTC 2024-Fall; and workshop chair for IEEE INFOCOM in 2018 and 2019.[6]

His CV separately lists 103 memberships in technical program committees from 2007 through 2026. They include repeated service for NeurIPS, ICLR, ICML, CVPR, ECCV, AAAI, IJCAI, IEEE INFOCOM, IEEE ICDCS, IEEE ICC, IEEE GLOBECOM, IEEE WCNC, and IEEE PerCom.[6]
