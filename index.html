Song Guo (Chinese: 郭嵩) is a computer scientist known for pioneering contributions to edge computing and edge intelligence.[1] He is a chair professor and associate head for research and knowledge transfer in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST).[2]

According to the IEEE Computer Society, he “pioneered Edge Learning, a new machine-learning paradigm for training and inference at the edge”.[3] In 2026, he was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society of Canada and received the CCF Award for Overseas Outstanding Contributions.[4][5][6]

Biography

Guo received a bachelor's degree from the Huazhong University of Science and Technology, a master's degree from the Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications, and a PhD in computer science from the University of Ottawa.[2][7] In 2006, he held a postdoctoral fellowship at the University of British Columbia.[8]

He was an assistant professor at the University of Northern British Columbia from 2006 to 2007. He worked at the University of Aizu from 2007 to 2016 and was a professor at the Hong Kong Polytechnic University from 2016 to 2023. He joined HKUST in 2023.[8] At HKUST, he also directs the Center of Physical AI.[7]

Research

Guo's research spans distributed and cloud computing, mobile computing, edge computing, and machine-learning systems.[2][7] His work on edge intelligence concerns machine-learning training and inference on edge servers and end devices.[9]

His more recent work includes edge-native foundation-model services and physical AI. His research on foundation-model services examines how distributed, heterogeneous edge resources can support personalized access to models with lower latency and greater privacy.[10] At HKUST, he directs the Pervasive Intelligence Laboratory, whose research includes world models, embodied AI, and foundation models for robotic agents interacting with physical environments.[2][11]

The IEEE Computer Society credits him with pioneering edge learning and recognized his pioneering contributions to edge computing and edge intelligence technologies with the 2024 Edward J. McCluskey Technical Achievement Award.[1][3] The Royal Society of Canada has described his foundational contributions as having established edge AI as a field.[5]

Guo was named a Clarivate Highly Cited Researcher in computer science for six consecutive years from 2020 through 2025.[12][13][14] His publications have received more than a dozen best-paper awards at IEEE and ACM venues.[2] Examples include an IEEE Computer Society Best Paper Award for research on computation offloading in edge computing using deep reinforcement learning,[15] and the 2019 IEEE Systems Journal Best Paper Award for a survey of energy-internet architectures and technologies.[16]

Professional service

In 2019, Guo was appointed the inaugural editor-in-chief of the IEEE Open Journal of the Computer Society, ahead of its launch in 2020.[17] He is editor-in-chief of IEEE Transactions on Cloud Computing.[18]

He has also served on the IEEE Communications Society Board of Governors and on IEEE Fellow evaluation committees for the Communications Society and the Computer Society.[2] He has served on the Engineering Panel for Joint Research Schemes of Hong Kong's Research Grants Council.[19] He was general co-chair of the 2023 IEEE Vehicular Technology Conference (VTC-Fall) and technical program co-chair of VTC2024-Fall.[20][21]
